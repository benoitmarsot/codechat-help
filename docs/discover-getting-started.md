# Getting Started with Discover

*A guide for newsroom staff.*

Discover is your newsroom's AI-powered research assistant. It lets you search and surface insights from your publication's archive — story leads, background context, source history, document patterns, and more — using plain-language questions instead of keyword searches.

This guide covers the three things you need to know to get the most out of Discover from day one.

## 1. How to write effective queries

Discover is designed to understand natural language, so you don't need to think in keywords. Ask it the way you'd ask a knowledgeable colleague.

The basics:

- Be specific about what you're looking for and why. Include context.
- State the subject, the angle, and the time frame when relevant.
- If you have a particular output in mind (a list of sources, a timeline, a summary), say so.

**Less effective:**

> City council water

**More effective:**

> What has the city council decided about water rates since 2020? I'm looking for key votes and any dissenting voices.

### Tips for stronger queries

- **Name names when you can.** People, places, agencies, and legislation produce tighter results than general topics.
- **Layer your context.** "For a story on rising rents" gives Discover more to work with than an unframed question.
- **Ask follow-up questions.** You don't need to get everything in one query. Refine and go deeper. Treat it like an interview — one question leads to another.
- **Request a format.** "Give me a bullet-point summary" or "List the five most relevant stories" will shape the response accordingly.
- **Try different angles.** If the first query doesn't land, rephrase. Approach the subject from a different direction — a source name, a date range, a related event.

## 2. Your archive: what's available

Discover draws from your publication's digitized archive. The depth and range of that archive will shape what's retrievable.

> Your archive covers material dating back to: **[INSERT DATE]**

A few things to keep in mind:

- **Older material may have variable quality.** Content digitized from print archives can have formatting or OCR inconsistencies that affect retrievability.
- **Recent content is typically most complete.** Material published in the last several years is generally well-indexed and highly searchable.
- **If something isn't surfacing, try broader terms.** Older stories may use terminology that differs from current usage.

When in doubt about what's in the archive, ask Discover directly — *"Do you have coverage of [topic] from [year range]?"* — and it will tell you what it can find.

## 3. Start a new discussion for each new topic

This is the single most important habit to develop.

Discover works within discussion threads — each conversation builds on what came before it. That's powerful when you're going deep on a single story or angle. But it becomes a problem when you mix topics.

**Why it matters:** When you ask about a new subject inside an existing discussion, Discover may blend context from the earlier conversation into its responses. This can produce results that seem subtly off — drawing from the wrong thread of your archive, or carrying assumptions that don't apply.

**The rule of thumb:** One story, one investigation, one question line = one discussion.

**How to do it:**

- Before starting a new query on a different topic, open a fresh discussion.
- Your discussion will automatically be named with the first query, but using the three dots next to the discussion in the left menu, you can rename and describe your discussion for clarity and future reference.
- You can always return to a prior discussion to pick up where you left off.

Think of each discussion as a dedicated research file. Keep it clean, keep it focused, and Discover will give you cleaner, more reliable results.

## Quick reference

| Goal | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Search the archive | Open a discussion, write a specific natural-language query |
| Get better results | Add context, names, dates, and a desired format |
| Research a new story | Start a new discussion — don't continue an old one |
| Check archive depth | Ask Discover directly what's available for a topic or date range |
| Refine a result | Ask a follow-up question in the same discussion |
